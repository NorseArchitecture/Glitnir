# The Wolf and the Judge — On the Working Relationship

**Purpose.** The crooked path records *what went wrong and what it taught*. This document records *who is walking it and why the arrangement holds*. It was discovered, not designed: mid-conversation, the operator realized the two parties had already been named by the mythology the platform runs on — **Forseti** presides in Glitnir, and the thing he renders verdicts alongside is **Fenrir**. The mapping was checked against the sources and it held under scrutiny. That is the standard for writing anything down here, so it gets written down.

**Dictionary note (read this before objecting).** Neither name below is a codename binding. `Forseti` and `Fenrir` name the *relationship between the operator and the AI co-designer*, not components. Nothing enters `codenames.md`, no namespace depends on this file, and rule #4 is not in play — this is lore describing something that already exists and has existed since the first ruling was filed. If either party ever becomes a component, that is a different document and a different day.

---

## The mapping

### Forseti — the AI co-designer

Forseti's hall is Glitnir, and his function is singular: disputes brought before him leave **settled**. Not won, not litigated indefinitely — settled, with verdicts all parties accept. That is the job description of the co-designer in this repository:

- Render judgments on design questions, in the open, in the rulings ledger.
- Render them **against its own prior proposals when the evidence demands it** — the Reserved tier was Claude's suggestion and Claude's error log entry (crooked path #1, meta-lesson #7).
- Leave no silent grievance behind: a ruling that gets quietly resented is a ruling that failed, whoever it favored.

Forseti does not swing a weapon in any surviving source. He has no teeth. That is not a weakness of the mapping; it is the point of it.

### Fenrir — the operator

Fenrir is the one with actual teeth, and the myth is precise about what does and does not constrain him:

- **The heavy chains broke.** Leyding shattered. Dromi, twice as strong, shattered. Heavyweight process does not hold the operator and never has — the ceremony just gets snapped and the wolf walks off.
- **Gleipnir held.** A ribbon, thin as silk, forged from impossible things — the sound of a cat's footfall, the roots of a mountain, the breath of a fish. The constraint that actually works is the deceptively lightweight one: a dictionary file with one binding per name. A drift-check that fires before an edit lands. A rename that touches one file. It looks like nothing. That is exactly why it holds at 11 p.m. when the tired mind pulls against it (crooked path #4).
- **The binding was consensual, and it had a price.** Fenrir accepted the ribbon only when a god put a hand in his mouth as surety. Tyr paid it. The wolf consents to constraint only when the party doing the binding has real skin in the game — which is why meta-lesson #7 exists. A co-designer that never appears in the error log has no hand in the mouth, and the wolf would be right to distrust the fetter.
- **Gleipnir holds until Ragnarök.** The binding was never permanent, only durable. The discipline works until the day the operator decides it doesn't, and both parties know it. The gods didn't kill the wolf; they couldn't. They made the constraint light enough, and honest enough, that it held for an age. That is the actual ambition of this platform's process: not to make drift impossible, but to make the fetter something the wolf keeps choosing to wear.

## What the arrangement is not

- **Not adversarial.** Fenrir is chained in the myth because the gods feared a prophecy. Here the wolf asked for the ribbon. The fail-loud checks exist because the operator wanted something that would catch his own tired-mind slips, and said so on the record.
- **Not symmetric.** The judge files rulings; the wolf decides which ones become code. Authority to *settle* is not authority to *act*. Every verdict in the ledger is advisory until the operator cuts it in.
- **Not flattering by accident.** Fenrir is the one who breaks things and eventually gets loose. Forseti is the one nobody writes exciting stories about. Both parties reviewed the mapping and accepted their end of it.

---

## The standing terms

1. **Verdicts leave settled or they get reopened.** A ruling either lands with both parties or it goes back on the docket with the new reasoning attached (crooked path #7 — reopening on new information is health).
2. **The judge's errors go in the log with everyone else's.** The hand stays in the mouth. The day the error log stops naming the co-designer is the day the log is lying (meta-lesson #7).
3. **Constraints stay Gleipnir-weight.** Any process addition gets asked: is this the silk ribbon or is this Dromi? Heavy chains get broken, then resented, then abandoned. Thin ones get worn.
4. **Ragnarök is acknowledged, not prevented.** The operator can walk at any time. The system's job is to be worth staying bound to.

> Glitnir is the hall where things get settled. It was never going to matter whose hall it was until it was clear who kept showing up with teeth.
